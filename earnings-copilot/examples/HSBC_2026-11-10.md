## 1. Results summary

The latest results are for 3Q26 and 9M26 (3Q26 release).

- **Headline profit is flattered by the base period; underlying growth is solid but modest.**
  - 3Q26 profit before tax (PBT) was $9.6bn, up 32% year on year. This was "primarily due to" the non-recurrence of $1.4bn of legal provisions in 3Q25.
  - On a constant currency basis excluding notable items, PBT was $10.0bn, up $0.9bn (about 10%).
  - For 9M26, reported PBT was $29.2bn (+26%) and underlying PBT was $30.4bn (+$2.0bn, +7%).
  - Annualised RoTE excluding notable items was 18.2% in 3Q26 and 18.8% for 9M26, against a target of "17% or better" (3Q26).

- **Revenue growth comes from banking NII and fees, but the margin dipped in the quarter.**
  - Revenue was $18.6bn (+5%). Banking NII was $11.7bn (+$0.6bn year on year).
  - Net interest margin (NIM) was 1.57%, flat year on year but **down 5bps from 1.62% in 2Q26**, "driven by AIEA increasing faster than NII". There was also a $0.1bn adverse one-off.
  - Wholesale Transaction Banking (WTB) fee and other income rose 11% in the quarter. Wealth was flat in 3Q because of Insurance one-offs and disposals, but up 13% over 9M26.
  - Banking NII guidance of "at least $46bn" was reiterated. With 9M26 at $34.6bn, this implies 4Q26 of at least about $11.4bn (my calculation), comfortably below the 3Q26 run-rate of $11.66bn.

- **Reported costs fell, but target-basis costs are running ahead of the full-year guide.**
  - Reported operating expenses were $8.8bn, down 12% because of the 3Q25 legal provisions.
  - Target-basis costs rose 1.4% in 3Q26 and 2.0% over 9M26, against "approximately 1%" growth guided for FY2026. Management adds that extra performance-related pay "would increase 2026 target basis cost growth modestly" (3Q26).
  - Meeting the guide therefore implies materially slower cost growth in 4Q26.

- **Credit costs are benign in the quarter but guidance implies a heavier 4Q.**
  - 3Q26 expected credit losses (ECL) were $0.8bn (31bps annualised). 9M26 ECL were $3.2bn (41bps), including a $0.5bn "fraud-related, secondary, securitisation exposure" in UK CIB.
  - Guidance remains "around 45bps" for 2026. Applying this to average gross loans including held for sale of roughly $1.04–1.05tn implies a full-year charge of about $4.7bn, or **a 4Q26 charge of roughly $1.5bn** (my calculation, approximate).

- **Capital is below the target range, yet buy-backs continue.**
  - The CET1 ratio was 13.9%, down from 14.1% at June and 14.9% at December 2025. The December fall reflects the Hang Seng Bank privatisation, which had a 110bps net impact, per the 20-F.
  - The ratio is below the 14–14.5% "medium-term target operating range". At 14%, CET1 capital would need to be about $1.0bn higher, given RWAs of $922.4bn and CET1 capital of $128.1bn (my calculation).
  - HSBC still completed a $1bn buy-back and announced another of up to $1bn. It now expects Basel 3.1 to add about 40–50bps on 1 January 2027.
  - RWAs rose $16.0bn in the quarter.

- **Unusual items to note.**
  - Higher yields drove a $2.6bn pre-tax loss on hold-to-collect-and-sell (HTCS) debt in 3Q26, taking the cumulative OCI loss to $5.0bn.
  - The unrecognised loss on hold-to-collect (amortised cost) debt grew by $4.9bn to $7.0bn.
  - Liquidity coverage ratio (LCR, 12-month average) eased to 132% from 134% in June and 137% in December. The leverage ratio was 4.9%, against a minimum of 3.25% plus a 1.0% buffer.
  - Cash flow is not a meaningful metric for a bank. The latest cash flow statement provided (1H26) shows $81.8bn of operating cash inflow, driven by deposit and balance sheet movements.

- **FY2025 context (calculated numbers).**
  - Revenue was $68.3bn (+3.7%) and NII $34.8bn (+6.3%). Net income fell 7.5% to $23.1bn because of notable items: $2.1bn of BoCom dilution and impairment, $1.4bn of legal provisions and $1.0bn of restructuring (20-F).

## 2. Management tone: latest results vs the previous ones

**Comparison:** 3Q26 Earnings Release (27 Oct 2026) vs 2026 Interim Results (4 Aug 2026). The 1H26 document is a fuller release with a CEO letter, so some topics are absent from 3Q26 because of format rather than intent.

**CEO framing has moved from triumphant to "navigating uncertainty".**
- 1H26: "HSBC is becoming the stronger bank we set out to build… The result is a bank capable of achieving more."
- 3Q26: "in a more complex world clients continue to turn to us to navigate uncertainty… As we simplify HSBC, we are creating greater capacity to invest, grow and deliver strong returns."
- Both remain confident ("pace, precision and discipline" appears in both). 3Q26 leans more on resilience in a harder environment.

**Guidance was held, not raised.**
- Banking NII:
  - 1H26 upgraded guidance to "at least $46bn" from "around $46bn". It cited "a continued favourable interest rate outlook, while recognising the outlook remains volatile and uncertain."
  - 3Q26 simply says "We continue to expect… at least $46bn". The rationale was dropped and the assumption date moved from "mid-July" to "mid-October 2026" market-implied rates.
- ECL: "around 45bps" in both. 1H26 added "reflecting ongoing uncertainty in the outlook". For context, the 20-F guided "around 40bps", so this guide was raised earlier in the year (the 1Q26 release was not provided).
- Costs: identical "approximately 1%" wording and the same caveat on extra performance-related pay. 3Q26 adds "As communicated at our 2026 interim results", signalling that this caveat is now more likely to bite.
- RoTE: "We remain confident" (≥17%) in both.

**Capital language has shifted.**
- 1H26: manage CET1 "within our medium-term target range of 14% to 14.5%".
- 3Q26: "target **operating** range". The release also introduces a quantified Basel 3.1 benefit ("approximately 40–50bps" at 1 January 2027), which was absent in 1H26.
- This reads as pre-emptive justification for continuing buy-backs below 14%. Note that the 20-F said buy-backs would not restart "until CET1 capital is back within, or above, this range".

**Risk language is more cautious on rates, sovereigns and credit.**
- New in 3Q26:
  - "the US Federal Reserve has raised its interest rate once".
  - "yields on long-term government debt have increased… amid concerns over fiscal outlooks".
  - Growing risks from non-bank financial institutions and private credit, and the risk of a "disruptive correction" in AI valuations.
- Downside ECL sensitivities rose versus December 2025. The 100% Downside 2 wholesale uplift went from $2.7bn to $4.2bn.
- Hong Kong commercial real estate (CRE) tone improved: "market conditions continued to stabilise". This contrasts with the 20-F's "office segment is still facing pressure".

**Topics that appeared or faded.**
- New in 3Q26:
  - Australia retail exit (sale to Blackstone affiliate; $25.6bn moved to held for sale).
  - Egypt retail sale.
  - The Singapore insurance sale terms (estimated $1.8bn gain).
  - A new strategic review of Türkiye retail and domestic SME banking.
  - The India Foreign Currency Non-Resident Deposit scheme as a balance sheet and RWA driver.
- Faded: the 1H26 CEO letter's emphasis on stablecoins, tokenised deposits and AI. This is likely a format effect.
- Simplification ambition is stable: about $2bn of annualised saves, with about $1.9bn actioned so far.

## 4. Analyst Q&A prep

**1. Your 9M26 ECL ratio is 41bps against guidance of "around 45bps". That implies roughly $1.5bn in 4Q. What do you expect to drive it?**
*Draft answer:*
- "We continue to expect an ECL charge… of around 45bps (including held for sale loan balances)". The 3Q26 charge was lower at 31bps, helped by lower Hong Kong CRE stage 3 charges and lower charges in UK and Mexico.
- We have added allowances for Middle East uncertainty. Downside scenario weights are elevated for the UK and France (15%) and the UAE (25%).
- Over the medium term we retain our 30–40bps planning range.

*(3Q26)*

**2. CET1 is 13.9%, below your 14–14.5% range, yet you have announced another $1bn buy-back. Why?**
*Draft answer:*
- The 0.2pt decline reflected higher RWAs (–0.3pt), HTCS fair value losses (–0.2pt) and the buy-back (–0.1pt). These were partly offset by 0.4pt of capital generation.
- "Based on our latest modelling", Basel 3.1 should benefit CET1 by "approximately 40–50bps" at 1 January 2027.
- "We intend to continue to manage our CET1 capital ratio within our medium-term target operating range of 14% to 14.5%."

*(3Q26)*

**3. NIM fell 5bps quarter on quarter. What is the 4Q and 2027 trajectory for banking NII, especially if rates rise?**
*Draft answer:*
- NII was stable quarter on quarter. The structural hedge reinvestment and a higher day count were offset by lower Markets Treasury NII and a $0.1bn one-off.
- NIM fell because "AIEA increasing faster than NII".
- We "continue to expect banking NII of at least $46bn in 2026", based on market-implied rates as of mid-October.
- We do not give 2027 NII guidance.

*(3Q26)*

**4. Target-basis costs are up 2.0% over 9M26 against a ~1% full-year guide. Is the guide still credible?**
*Draft answer:*
- "The Group remains on track to deliver… approximately 1% in 2026 on a target basis." 9M26 growth partly reflects the "phasing of the performance-related pay accrual".
- Simplification reduced income-statement costs by $0.8bn in 9M26. About $1.9bn of annualised saves have been actioned.
- Additional performance-related pay "would increase 2026 target basis cost growth modestly".

*(3Q26)*

**5. Unrealised losses on your bond portfolios rose sharply: $5.0bn on HTCS and $7.0bn unrecognised on amortised cost. What are the capital and liquidity implications if yields keep rising?**
*Draft answer:*
- These portfolios are held "as part of our interest rate hedging strategy and liquidity management". The HTCS loss includes related fair value hedge movements and accounted for 0.2pt of the CET1 decline.
- The amortised-cost portfolio is held to collect.
- The documents do not give further sensitivity or quantify a liquidity impact, so management would need to add detail here.

*(3Q26)*

**6. The Luxembourg Court of Appeal ruled against HSSL again in July. Is the $1.1bn Herald provision still adequate?**
*Draft answer:*
- "HSSL continues to recognise a $1.1bn provision." HSSL "has a right to seek a further appeal to the Luxembourg Court of Cassation", and quantum will be determined in further proceedings.
- "Given the complexities and uncertainties associated with determining the quantum of restitution, the eventual financial impact could be significantly different."

*(1H26, Note 7)*

**7. What is the net P&L and capital effect of the disposal pipeline, and how does it feed into distributions?**
*Draft answer:*

- A capital release figure is not disclosed.

**8. How durable and capital-efficient is the deposit and lending growth tied to India's FCNR scheme, and what about CIB outflows and Hong Kong deposit pricing?**

## 5. Company brief

HSBC is a London-headquartered global bank with $3.4tn of assets (3Q26). Since 2025 it has been organised into four businesses plus Corporate Centre:

- **Hong Kong:** retail, wealth and commercial banking, including the now wholly owned Hang Seng Bank. 9M26 PBT was $7.7bn, with RoTE of 43.3%.
- **UK:** ring-fenced retail and commercial bank. 9M26 PBT was $5.2bn.
- **Corporate and Institutional Banking:** transaction banking, markets and financing. It is the largest segment, with 9M26 PBT of $10.9bn.
- **International Wealth and Premier Banking:** Private Bank, Asset Management, Insurance and Premier outside the home markets. 9M26 PBT was $3.7bn.

Corporate Centre holds associates including BoCom and Saudi Awwal Bank (3Q26).

Banking NII made up about 61% of 9M26 underlying revenue, at $34.6bn of $56.8bn (my calculation). The rest is fees and other income, principally WTB ($8.9bn) and Wealth ($8.2bn) (3Q26).

**Strategy:**
- Simplify the group (about $2bn of annualised saves).
- Exit non-strategic markets (Australia retail, Malta, Indonesia, Egypt, Singapore insurance) and redeploy about $1.8bn of costs into Wealth and Hong Kong.
- Deliver RoTE of ≥17% excluding notable items over 2026–28, a 50% target-basis payout and revenue growth rising to 5% by 2028 (3Q26).

**Main risks:**
- Geopolitics, particularly the Middle East conflict and US–China tensions.
- Interest rate and sovereign-yield volatility, which hits NII and bond valuations.
- Hong Kong CRE.
- Private-credit and fraud exposures.
- Legal matters, especially Madoff/Herald.
- Execution risk on multiple disposals.
- Capital headroom while operating below the CET1 target range (3Q26; 1H26).

## 6. Questions to ask in an interview

1. **On capital:** HSBC is buying back stock at 13.9% CET1, below its own 14% floor, and is leaning on a 40–50bps Basel 3.1 benefit from January 2027. How do the teams you work with think about the risk that model refinements or supervisory permissions shrink that benefit?

2. **On credit:** The 9M26 charge included a $0.5bn fraud-related securitisation exposure to a financial sponsor, and the risk section flags private-credit interconnectedness. Has this changed underwriting or monitoring of sponsor and fund-finance lending in CIB?

3. **On Hong Kong:** Hong Kong deposits grew $20.6bn in the quarter, mostly term deposits, while management cites margin pressure from competition. Following the Hang Seng privatisation, how is the bank balancing deposit growth against margin, and where are the $0.5bn of synergies expected to come from first?

4. **On rates:** Higher yields support the structural hedge, but they drove a $4.9bn quarterly rise in unrecognised losses on hold-to-collect bonds. How does Treasury decide how much of the hedge and liquidity book sits in HTC versus HTCS?

5. **On portfolio reshaping:** With Australia, Egypt, Indonesia, Malta and Singapore insurance exiting and a new review in Türkiye, which international markets does the bank see as core to IWPB's growth? How is the $105bn of 9M26 net new money, of which $95bn was in Asia, being converted into fee income?
