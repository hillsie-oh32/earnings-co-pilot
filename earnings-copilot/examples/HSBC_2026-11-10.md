# HSBC Holdings plc: briefing pack (3Q26 results)

*Reporting currency is USD. "3Q26 ER" is the Earnings Release 3Q26 (6-K, 27 Oct 2026). "1H26" is the 2026 Interim results (6-K, 4 Aug 2026). "20-F" is the FY2025 Form 20-F. "Calc" is the calculated numbers.*

---

## 1. Results summary

- **Statutory profit up sharply, mostly from easy comparisons; the underlying picture is solid.**
  - 3Q26 profit before tax (PBT) was $9.6bn, up 32%. The main driver was the absence of the $1.4bn legal provisions booked in 3Q25.
  - Excluding notable items, at constant currency, PBT was $10.0bn, up $0.9bn.
  - 9M26 PBT was $29.2bn (+26%).
  - Annualised RoTE for 9M26 was 18.0%, or 18.8% excluding notable items, against a target of "17% or better" (3Q26 ER).
  - For context, FY2025 net income fell 7.5% to $23.1bn while revenue grew 3.7% (Calc). That fall was driven by notable items such as the BoCom dilution and impairment ($2.1bn) and legal provisions ($1.4bn) (20-F).
- **Revenue and margin.**
  - 3Q26 revenue was $18.6bn (+5%). Banking NII was $11.7bn (+5%).
  - NIM was 1.57%, flat year on year but **down 5bps on 2Q26 (1.62%)**, "driven by AIEA increasing faster than NII".
  - Fee and other income in Wholesale Transaction Banking (WTB) rose 11%. CIB Debt and Equity Markets rose 70%. Wealth fee income was flat on a reported basis, held back by disposals (3Q26 ER).
- **Costs under control.**
  - Reported operating expenses were $8.8bn (−12%, helped by the legal provisions not recurring).
  - Target-basis costs, which exclude notable items, rose 1.4% in 3Q and 2.0% in 9M.
  - Cost efficiency ratio was 47.4% (3Q26 ER).
- **Credit cost was lower in the quarter, but guidance implies a heavier 4Q.**
  - 3Q26 expected credit losses (ECL) were $0.8bn, or 31bps annualised. The 9M figure was 41bps.
  - Full-year guidance of ~45bps was retained, which implies 4Q charges well above 3Q.
  - The 9M charge included a $0.5bn fraud-related securitisation exposure in UK CIB (3Q26 ER).
- **Capital below target, yet buy-backs continue.**
  - CET1 fell to **13.9%**, below the 14–14.5% target range. The movement was −0.3pt from RWAs, −0.2pt from losses on hold-to-collect-and-sell (HTCS) bonds, −0.1pt from the buy-back, and +0.4pt from profits net of dividends.
  - A further $1bn buy-back and a $0.10 dividend were announced.
  - Management expects Basel 3.1 to add ~40–50bps to CET1 from 1 Jan 2027 (3Q26 ER).
- **Liquidity and funding remain strong, though LCR is drifting down.**
  - LCR was 132%, down from 134% at 2Q26 and 137% at December 2025.
  - The loan-to-deposit ratio was 56.1%. Customer accounts were $1.84tn (3Q26 ER).
  - Cash flow is not a meaningful lens for a bank. For reference, 1H26 operating cash flow was $81.8bn, and the Hang Seng privatisation consumed $13.6bn of financing cash (1H26).
- **Unusual items.**
  - The pre-tax unrealised loss on HTCS bonds widened to $5.0bn (2Q26: $2.4bn).
  - The unrecognised loss on amortised-cost bonds widened to $7.0bn, a $4.9bn deterioration in the quarter, as yields rose.
  - $25.6bn of Australian retail loans moved to held for sale (3Q26 ER).

## 2. Management tone: latest results vs the previous ones

This compares the 3Q26 ER with the 1H26 interim.

**Headline language: confident in both, but framed more around uncertainty in 3Q26.**
- 1H26 was triumphal: "HSBC is becoming the stronger bank we set out to build… a bank capable of achieving more."
- 3Q26 leads with the environment: "in a more complex world clients continue to turn to us to navigate uncertainty".
- The execution mantra is unchanged ("pace, precision and discipline").

**Guidance: held, not upgraded.**
- Banking NII:
  - At FY2025 the guidance was "at least $45bn" (20-F).
  - 1H26 raised it to "at least $46bn" (from "around $46bn"), "reflecting a continued favourable interest rate outlook".
  - 3Q26 simply "continue[s] to expect" at least $46bn, and drops the rate-outlook rationale.
  - With 9M banking NII at $34.6bn, the guide implies a 4Q below the 3Q run-rate, so it looks conservative.
- ECL guidance has drifted upward over the year:
  - It was ~40bps at FY2025 (20-F) and ~45bps by 1H26.
  - 1H26 said this reflected "ongoing uncertainty in the outlook"; 3Q26 repeats ~45bps without that caveat.
- Cost guidance (~1% target-basis growth) is unchanged. Both releases keep the caveat about "additional performance-related pay".

**Capital: the most material shift.**
- 1H26 (CET1 14.1%): "manage the CET1 capital ratio within our medium-term target range".
- 3Q26 (13.9%): "manage our CET1 capital ratio within our medium-term target operating range". Despite being below that range, it launches another $1bn buy-back.
- Basel 3.1 ("a benefit of approximately 40–50bps") is a **new topic** and is clearly being relied on.
- Bull reading: on management's modelling, Basel 3.1 restores CET1 to the range within a quarter.
- Bear reading: capital discipline is being loosened ahead of an uncertain regulatory outcome.

**New or heavier risk language in 3Q26.**
- On rates: "the US Federal Reserve has raised its interest rate once, while the BoE has left its policy rate unchanged".
- On sovereign debt and non-bank financial institutions: "may amplify the transmission of market volatility".
- A "disruptive correction to the valuations of AI and technology-related assets".
- Deposit competition: "lower average margins on time deposits due to market competition" (Hong Kong) and "growing competition for retail deposits" (UK).

**Hong Kong commercial real estate (CRE).** The tone has softened. 1H26 cited the "varying pace of recovery"; 3Q26 says conditions "continued to stabilise" while flagging "valuation pressure" in non-prime segments.

**Portfolio actions.** New disclosures are the Türkiye retail and SME strategic review, the Australia retail exit, and the Egypt sale. The Singapore life sale ($1.8bn expected gain) was first disclosed as a post-balance-sheet event in 1H26.

- **Return on equity.** Calc shows 11.7%, versus the company's RoE of 12.3% (20-F). The company measure is profit attributable to ordinary shareholders over average ordinary equity, so the two differ in definition. I use the company's figures.

## 4. Analyst Q&A prep

**1. CET1 is 13.9%, below your 14–14.5% range, yet you have launched another $1bn buy-back. Why?**
> "We expect Basel 3.1 to benefit CET1 by approximately 40–50bps at 1 January 2027. We generated 0.4pt of CET1 from profits net of dividends in 3Q, and we intend to manage within our 14–14.5% target operating range." (3Q26 ER)

**2. 9M ECL is 41bps, but you guide to ~45bps. What drives a heavier 4Q?**
> "Our guidance reflects allowances for uncertainty from the Middle East conflict and continued pressure in non-prime Hong Kong CRE. Scenario weights for the UK, France and the UAE are tilted to the downside, and Downside 2 sensitivity rose to $4.2bn wholesale. Medium-term planning remains 30–40bps." (3Q26 ER)

**3. NIM fell 5bps quarter on quarter. Can banking NII keep growing?**
> "NII was stable quarter on quarter. Structural hedge reinvestment and day count offset lower Markets Treasury NII and a $0.1bn one-off. NIM fell because AIEA grew faster than NII. We continue to expect banking NII of at least $46bn, based on mid-October market rates." (3Q26 ER)

**4. Rising yields drove $2.6bn of HTCS losses in 3Q and a $4.9bn deterioration in hold-to-collect marks. What is the capital sensitivity?**
> "These portfolios support interest rate hedging and liquidity. The HTCS movement cost 0.2pt of CET1 in the quarter. Hold-to-collect marks are not recognised in capital." (3Q26 ER) Sensitivity beyond this is not disclosed.

**5. What happened with the $0.5bn securitisation fraud, and how big is your exposure to financial sponsors and private credit?**
> "This was a single fraud-related, secondary securitisation exposure with a financial sponsor in UK CIB." (3Q26 ER) A sector exposure figure is not disclosed in the documents, so management would need to supply it.

**6. Will extra performance pay break the ~1% cost target?**
> "Should strong business performance continue, we may consider additional performance-related pay, which would increase 2026 target basis cost growth modestly. Simplification has actioned ~$1.9bn of annualised savings against a ~$2bn goal." (3Q26 ER)

**7. IWPB 3Q PBT fell 14% and Wealth fees were flat. Is Wealth momentum slowing?**
> "The decline reflected the non-recurrence of one-off favourable Insurance impacts in 3Q25 and $0.1bn of strategic-transaction drag. Investment Distribution and Private Bank grew. Net new money was $41bn in 3Q and $105bn in 9M." (3Q26 ER)

**8. How should we model the disposal pipeline and Türkiye?**
> "We expect pre-tax gains of ~$1.8bn on HSBC Life Singapore (completion 1H27), ~$0.3bn on Egypt retail (2H27) and up to $0.4bn on Indonesia retail (1H27). Australia carries $0.3bn of restructuring cost and $0.2bn of FX reserve losses to recycle. The Türkiye retail and SME review has no conclusion yet." (3Q26 ER)

## 5. Company brief

HSBC is a London-headquartered universal bank with $3.4tn of assets. It is deposit-rich, with customer accounts of $1.84tn against loans of $1.03tn (3Q26 ER).

**Segments (9M26 PBT, 3Q26 ER):**
- **Hong Kong** (retail, wealth and commercial, including the now fully owned Hang Seng Bank): $7.7bn. RoTE is 43%.
- **UK** (ring-fenced retail and commercial): $5.2bn.
- **Corporate and Institutional Banking**: $10.9bn. This covers transaction banking (FX, payments, trade, securities services) and markets.
- **International Wealth and Premier Banking** (Private Bank, Asset Management, Insurance): $3.7bn.
- **Corporate Centre**: mainly associates such as BoCom and SAB.

**How it makes money.** Banking NII ($34.6bn in 9M26) dominates and is sensitive to rates; a structural hedge smooths it. Fee and other income ($22.3bn), mainly from wealth and WTB, is the growth engine.

**Strategy.**
- Simplify into four businesses, targeting ~$2bn of savings.
- Exit sub-scale markets (Australia, Malta, Egypt and others).
- Redeploy ~$1.8bn of costs into Hong Kong, wealth and transaction banking.
- Targets: RoTE ≥17% (2026–28), revenue growth rising to 5% in 2028, and a 50% payout (3Q26 ER).

**Main risks.**
- Rates and NIM sensitivity.
- Credit: Hong Kong CRE, Middle East and UK exposures.
- Capital headroom and execution on Basel 3.1.
- Bond mark-to-market losses.
- Geopolitics and sanctions (US–China).
- Madoff-related litigation.
- Execution risk across multiple disposals.

## 6. Questions to ask in an interview

1. CET1 is at 13.9% and below target, yet buy-backs continue on the strength of an expected 40–50bp Basel 3.1 benefit. How much of that benefit do you see as dependent on supervisory permissions, and how would capital return change if it came in lower?
2. Credit-impaired Hong Kong CRE balances are falling, but LTVs on those loans have risen to 83% from 71% since December. Is that a sign recoveries will be lower than provisioned, or a lagged collateral revaluation?
3. NIM fell 5bps quarter on quarter while AIEA grew, partly through India FCNR-related sovereign assets. How do you think about growing the balance sheet in lower-margin assets versus protecting margin?
4. The $0.5bn sponsor-linked securitisation fraud comes as management itself highlights private-market interconnectedness. What changed in underwriting or monitoring of financial-sponsor exposures afterwards?
5. Hong Kong time-deposit margins and UK retail deposit competition are both flagged. How durable is HSBC's deposit franchise advantage if rates stay higher for longer?
