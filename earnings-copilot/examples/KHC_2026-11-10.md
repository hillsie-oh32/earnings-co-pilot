# Kraft Heinz (KHC): Q3 2026 Briefing Pack

*Sources: Q3 2026 earnings release (4 Nov 2026), "Q3 PR"; Q2 2026 earnings release (5 Aug 2026), "Q2 PR"; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, "10-Q"; and the calculated numbers and red-flag scan, "Calc". Figures are in US$. Where I have done my own arithmetic, I label it as an estimate.*

---

## 1. Results summary

- **Sales are still shrinking, with emerging markets the only growth.** Q3 net sales fell 2.0% to $6,115m. Organic sales fell 1.1%, with price flat and volume/mix down 1.1pp (Q3 PR).
  - About 80bp of the volume decline came from retailers pulling orders forward into Q2. Q2 had booked a matching ~80bp benefit (Q2 PR), so over the two quarters the effect roughly cancels out.
  - North America organic sales fell 3.2%. Both price (−0.8pp, from "price investments in cream cheese and meats") and volume (−2.4pp) declined (10-Q MD&A).
  - Emerging Markets organic sales grew 15.6%.
- **Profit is falling about 15% as the company reinvests.**
  - Adjusted Operating Income fell 15.2% to $939m. Adjusted EPS fell 13.1% to $0.53. GAAP diluted EPS was $0.46, down 11.5% (Q3 PR).
  - Drivers given: inflation in procurement and logistics, higher variable pay, weaker volume/mix and more advertising.
  - Europe and Pacific Developed Markets (EPDM) segment profit fell 32.8% to $96m, partly due to "spare parts write offs" (10-Q).
- **The gross margin headline flatters.** GAAP gross margin rose 60bp to 32.5%. That was helped by a $39m unrealised gain on commodity hedges, against a $23m loss a year ago. On the adjusted basis, gross margin *fell* 40bp to 31.9% (Q3 PR, Schedules 7 and 11).
- **Cash flow weakened in Q3.**
  - Year to date, operating cash flow was $2,960m (−4.1%) and free cash flow (FCF) was $2,361m (−5.2%). FCF conversion (FCF as a share of adjusted net income) was 120% (Q3 PR).
  - Q3 alone: FCF was $702m against $986m a year earlier (Calc), about −29%. At the half year, FCF had been *up* 10.3% (Q2 PR).
  - Working capital has been helped by payables. The payables inflow was $618m year to date against $312m last year. Amounts under the supplier-finance programme rose to $892m from $755m (10-Q, Note 13).
- **Guidance was nudged up, but the operating outlook is unchanged.**
  - Organic sales guidance is now −0.5% to −1.25% (low end raised from −2%).
  - Adjusted EPS guidance is now $2.07–2.13 (was $2.03–2.09).
  - Constant-currency Adjusted Operating Income guidance is unchanged at −16% to −18%. Adjusted gross margin is now expected "at the low end" of the −10bp to −50bp range (Q3 PR).
  - My estimate: most of the 4c midpoint EPS raise comes from the lower tax rate (~23.5% vs ~24.5%) and higher other income ($210m vs $200m), not from operations.
- **Unusual items.**
  - $7.4bn of non-cash impairments year to date, almost all booked in Q2.
  - About $3bn of net debt reduction. Total debt fell from $21.2bn to $18.0bn, helped by a tender offer that bought back bonds below par for a $264m gain (10-Q).
  - A North America coffee divestiture was signed on 3 Nov at a $650m enterprise value, with KHC keeping a 30% stake. It appears in the 10-Q but not in the press release.
- **The guidance implies a weak Q4 (my estimate).**
  - Adjusted EPS: full-year guidance less the nine-month result of $1.67 implies Q4 of $0.40–0.46, against $0.53 in Q3.
  - FCF: ~110% conversion on full-year adjusted net income (using about 1,186m shares) implies Q4 FCF of roughly $0.3–0.4bn, against $1,171m in Q4 2025 (Calc).

## 3. Red flags explained

**[RED] Leverage: net debt but negative EBITDA. Verdict: largely an accounting artefact, but leverage still deserves attention.**
- **Why EBITDA is negative.** The 12-month operating loss (−$3,327m, Calc) comes from $7,437m of non-cash goodwill and brand impairments year to date (10-Q, Note 7).
  - Excluding them, nine-month Adjusted Operating Income was $3,038m and depreciation and amortisation (D&A) $768m (Q3 PR).
  - My rough proxy for nine-month EBITDA is therefore about $3.8bn. This is not the company's Adjusted EBITDA, which is defined but not quantified in these documents.
  - Annualised, that puts net debt of $16.1bn at roughly 3.2x.
- **Mitigants (all 10-Q):**
  - Total debt fell from $21.2bn to $18.0bn.
  - The $4.0bn revolver is undrawn and was extended to 2031.
  - The company was in covenant compliance.
- **Concerns:**
  - The underlying earnings that service the debt are falling about 15%.
  - S&P and Fitch have KHC at BBB with a *negative* outlook (10-Q).

**[AMBER] Cash conversion: loss over the last 12 months. Verdict: innocent on headline, but cash quality is worth questioning.**
- The statutory loss is again driven by impairments. Against adjusted net income of $1,972m, FCF conversion was 120% (Q3 PR, Schedule 16).
- However, cash generation leans on factors that may not repeat:
  - The payables inflow ($618m vs $312m). Q2 PR cited "improved payments terms".
  - Supplier finance up $137m.
  - Supplier payment terms of up to 250 days (10-Q, Note 13).
  - Lower cash taxes from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, the 2025 US tax law), which "is expected to reduce cash tax payments in 2026" but not in later years (10-Q).
- Q3 standalone FCF fell about 29%, and the implied Q4 is weak (Section 1).

**[AMBER] Goodwill is 55% of equity. Verdict: concerning (non-cash, but further write-downs are plausible).**
- Goodwill of $19.6bn plus indefinite-lived brands of $29.2bn together exceed shareholders' equity of $36.0bn (10-Q).
- Headroom is thin (10-Q, Note 7 and MD&A):
  - Four reporting units holding $18.2bn of goodwill have less than 5% fair value over carrying amount: Taste Elevation (TE), Hydration, Desserts & Meals (HDM), Europe, and Away From Home (AFH).
  - $9.3bn of brands (Kraft, Oscar Mayer, Lunchables and others) are at *zero* headroom.
  - A 50bp rise in the discount rate would cut reporting-unit fair value by $3.0bn.
- The 10-Q warns that "decisions to divest certain non-strategic assets could lead to future goodwill impairments." That is relevant to the coffee sale: coffee sits within the Meat, Cheese, Coffee & Snacks (MCCS) unit, which has 5–10% headroom.
- Context (my inference): Q3 buybacks averaged about $25.60 a share (10-Q, Item 2). Across about 1,184m shares, that is roughly $30bn of market value, below book equity.

**Beyond the scan:**
- **Going concern:** none.
- **Material weakness:** none. Disclosure controls were "effective" (10-Q, Item 4). A multi-year ERP (finance systems) migration is ongoing.
- **Auditor changes or restatements:** none, per the Calc filing history.
- **IRS transfer-pricing dispute (material).**
  - Notices of Proposed Adjustment for 2018–2022 imply about $1.02bn of additional federal tax, plus about $85m of penalties per year (roughly $425m in total), excluding interest. My summation of 10-Q Note 8.
  - **No reserve has been recorded.** The company "strongly disagree[s]".
  - KHC keeps the same transfer-pricing model, so exposure may continue beyond 2022.
- **Derivative litigation.** The 3G-related suit was revived by the Delaware Supreme Court (June 2025). A further amended complaint was sought in Sept 2026, and the company "cannot reasonably estimate" any loss.
- **Environmental cases.** A Brazilian matter now includes a criminal complaint, and there is a Clean Water Act matter at Kendallville. Management expects neither to be material.
- **Gap between adjusted and statutory profit.** Nine-month GAAP EPS was −$3.47 against adjusted EPS of $1.67. Most of the $5.14 gap is impairments ($5.30).
  - The adjustments also *remove* gains: the $264m debt gain (−$0.14) and $116m of unrealised hedge gains (−$0.07).
  - However, $90m of separation costs ($24m in Q3) are still being excluded even though the separation was paused on 11 Feb 2026.
- **Minor discrepancy.** The tender gain is $265m in 10-Q Note 14 and $264m in the cash flow statement and Schedule 9. I use $264m: the year-to-date figure net of a $1m Q3 cost. Similarly, the $79m of cash buybacks offset share dilution and tax withholding; none were bought under the $3bn programme.

## 4. Analyst Q&A prep

**1. The guidance implies Q4 adjusted EPS of $0.40–0.46, below Q3's $0.53. And the EPS raise came with an unchanged operating income outlook. What is driving this?**
Draft answer:
- Operating income guidance is unchanged at −16% to −18% in constant currency.
- Gross margin is expected at the low end of the range.
- The outlook includes ~$700m of incremental investment and a ~500bp headwind from lapping lower incentive pay.
- The EPS raise reflects a ~23.5% tax rate and ~$210m of other income (Q3 PR).
- We expect Q4 inflation to increase, partly due to the Iran conflict (10-Q MD&A).

**2. North America volume/mix was −2.4pp and price −0.8pp. Are you buying volume with price, and when does volume turn positive?**
Draft answer:
- About 80bp of group volume was a reversal of the Q2 pull-forward. Retailers "accelerated purchases into the second quarter and further reduced inventory in the third quarter."
- Price investment was targeted at cream cheese and meats, with higher pricing in refreshment beverages (10-Q).
- Share, sales and volume trends are improving in every region.
- The outlook includes ~100bp of SNAP headwinds (Q3 PR).

**3. Why has gross margin moved to the low end of guidance?**
Draft answer:
- Procurement and logistics inflation increased (Q3 PR).
- Costs fell year to date for cheese, dairy, meats and coffee, but rose for edible oils, resins, plastics, and fruit and vegetables.
- We mitigate through efficiency, pricing, alternative sourcing and hedging, but timing can lag (10-Q MD&A).
- Any recovery of overturned tariffs depends on suppliers, because we are mostly not the Importer of Record (10-Q).

**4. How sustainable is the 120% FCF conversion, given payables and supplier-finance tailwinds and the weak Q3?**
Draft answer:
- Working capital benefited from payables, partly offset by inventory rebuilding, higher input costs and advertising outflows (Q3 PR).
- Supplier-finance participation is voluntary, and we pay on original invoice terms (10-Q, Note 13).
- We guide to ~110% conversion for the year and capex of ~$800m (Q3 PR; 10-Q).

**5. With $18.2bn of goodwill under 5% headroom and $9.3bn of brands at zero, how should investors think about further impairments, including from the coffee sale?**
Draft answer:
- The impairments are non-cash.
- The Q2 charges reflected "the market's perceived risk" of achieving the plan, plus updated cash flows that include our investment programme.
- The annual test as of 28 June 2026 found no further impairment.
- We disclose sensitivities and note that divestitures could trigger future impairments (10-Q, Note 7).

**6. What is the strategy behind selling coffee and keeping 30%, and what does it mean for the paused separation?**
Draft answer:
- The coffee agreement values the business at $650m enterprise value. We retain a 30% non-controlling stake, and closing is expected in mid-2027, subject to approvals (10-Q, Note 4).
- The Board paused separation work on 11 Feb 2026. If resumed, it remains subject to Board approval, tax opinions and SEC filings (10-Q MD&A).
- *No financial impact of the sale has been disclosed.*

**7. Capital allocation: debt reduction versus the dividend versus buybacks, given BBB/negative outlooks?**
Draft answer:
- Year to date we have repaid $1.9bn of maturing notes, tendered $1.4bn of 2046 notes and redeemed $1.0bn of 2027 notes.
- Total debt is down to $18.0bn.
- The Q4 dividend of $0.40 has been declared.
- No shares were bought under the programme, which has ~$1.5bn of authorisation remaining through 26 Dec 2026 (10-Q).
- The cash flow profile supports "investing in the business, sustaining the dividend, and reducing debt" (Q3 PR).

**8. Why did Europe and Pacific Developed Markets profit fall 32.8% while Emerging Markets rose 22.5%? Is EM growth pricing-led and sustainable?**
Draft answer:
- **EPDM:**
  - Profit fell on variable pay, spare-parts write-offs and advertising.
  - Volume was hit by portfolio simplification and competition in Australia and New Zealand.
  - The UK and Germany grew (10-Q).
- **EM:**
  - Organic growth was +15.6% (7.2pp price, 8.4pp volume), driven by Indonesia, Brazil and Venezuela.
  - Pricing was taken to offset inflation.
  - Venezuela, Turkey and Egypt use highly inflationary accounting (10-Q).

## 5. Company brief

Kraft Heinz is a global packaged-food company with about $25bn of 2025 net sales (Q3 PR). Brands include Heinz, Kraft, Philadelphia, Oscar Mayer, Lunchables, Capri-Sun and Maxwell House.

**How it makes money.** It sells branded grocery products, mostly through retailers, plus away-from-home channels.
- **Segments (nine-month 2026):** North America $13.6bn (~74%), Europe and Pacific Developed Markets $2.8bn (~15%), Emerging Markets $2.1bn (~11%) (10-Q).
- **Largest category:** condiments, sauces and spreads at $8.3bn, about 45% of sales.

**Strategy.**
- Turn around the US business and accelerate international growth (Q3 PR).
- Fund this with ~$700m of incremental 2026 investment in marketing, sales and R&D.
- Return to "volume-led, sustainable and profitable growth" (Q2 PR).
- Portfolio pruning: Italy infant food sold; North America coffee being sold.
- The planned split into two companies is paused.

**Main risks.**
- Persistent volume declines in North America and private-label competition.
- SNAP benefit cuts (~100bp headwind).
- Input-cost inflation, including from the Iran conflict and tariffs.
- Further impairments, given thin headroom on $19.6bn of goodwill and $29.2bn of brands.
- $18.0bn of debt with negative rating outlooks.
- A ~$1bn-plus unreserved IRS transfer-pricing dispute.
- The influence of the largest stockholder, and separation uncertainty (10-Q).
