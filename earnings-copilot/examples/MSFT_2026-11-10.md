# Microsoft Corp (MSFT): briefing pack, Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 30 September 2026)

## 1. Results summary

- **Growth was strong and driven by cloud.** Revenue was $92.3bn, up 19% (19% in constant currency). This is the fastest year-on-year rate in the eight-quarter table (18.8%).
  - Agents and Infra grew 25% to $76.8bn.
  - Microsoft Cloud grew 30% to $63.9bn.
  - Azure grew 48% to $33.2bn, adding $10.8bn year on year.
  - Devices and Consumer fell 3% to $15.5bn, with Windows OEM and devices down 22% (press release; 10-Q Note 16).
- **Operating margin was the highest of the eight quarters, but gross margin is eroding.**
  - Operating margin was 49.8% (48.9% a year earlier). Operating expenses rose only 6%, sales and marketing was flat and headcount fell 2% (10-Q MD&A).
  - Gross margin was 67.8%, down from 69.0%. Microsoft Cloud gross margin "decreased to 66% driven by sales mix shift to Azure" (10-Q).
  - Depreciation was $11.1bn versus $7.1bn a year earlier (10-Q Note 6), so cost pressure is building.
- **GAAP earnings growth is flattered by last year's low base.**
  - GAAP net income rose 32% to $36.6bn, mainly because the prior-year quarter included a $3.1bn after-tax loss from OpenAI.
  - Non-GAAP net income, which excludes OpenAI, rose 18% to $36.4bn, with EPS of $4.89 up 18%. This is the better measure of the underlying trend.
  - The effective tax rate was 20% (19% a year earlier).
- **Free cash flow fell sharply because capex roughly doubled.**
  - Operating cash flow was $58.0bn, up $12.9bn. Additions to property and equipment were $39.4bn, up from $19.4bn.
  - FCF fell to $18.6bn from $25.7bn. On a last-twelve-months basis, FCF was about $60.0bn against $78.0bn the year before (down about 23%), even though operating cash flow rose 33% (calculated numbers).
  - Cash and short-term investments fell $12.3bn in the quarter to $64.5bn (10-Q).
- **Backlog is very large but flat on the quarter.** Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO) was $677bn, up 73% year on year. It was $678bn at June, when growth was 84% (both press releases). About 30% is expected to be recognised within 12 months (10-Q Note 11).
- **No guidance appears in the documents.** Guidance is given on the call. The release states results "exceeded expectations across revenue, operating income, and earnings per share."
- **Unusual items:**
  - OpenAI was a related-party customer contributing $10.0bn of revenue, about 11% of the total, with $7.2bn of receivables (10-Q Note 1).
  - Segments were reorganised and prior periods recast.
  - Datacenter leases signed but not yet commenced total $364.6bn (10-Q Note 12).

*Notes on figures that differ between sources:*
- **OpenAI gain.** It is $272m pre-tax in other income and $207m after $65m tax in net income (10-Q non-GAAP table). I use $207m when discussing earnings.
- **Shareholder returns.** The "$10.2bn returned" equals dividends declared ($7,279m) plus programme buybacks ($2,925m). The cash flow statement shows $11.1bn paid: dividends paid of $6,757m plus repurchases of $4,379m, which include $1.5bn of shares bought back to settle employee tax withholding. I use $10.2bn when quoting management.

**Interpretation**
- **More confident on execution.** The beat needed no caveats this time, and Amy Hood explicitly claims acceleration.
- **Less promotional about AI volume.** Copilot seat numbers have disappeared from the release. The 10-Q now only says growth was driven by "upsell to premium seat offerings, including Microsoft 365 Copilot, E5, and E7".
- **Shift towards enterprise control and governance.** The CEO's language now stresses "choice", "control" and "trust". This fits the new segment description's "multi-model system" (10-Q Note 1). Reading this as reduced emphasis on any single model partner is my interpretation, not a stated position.

**Like-for-like caution**
The comparisons are not clean because Microsoft redefined its metrics this quarter. The 10-Q says the Azure, Microsoft 365 commercial cloud and search metrics were updated "for the movement of certain components".
- Q4's "Azure and other cloud services +43%" is not directly comparable with Q1's "Azure +48%".
- The claim that Microsoft Cloud growth "accelerat[ed] to 30%" cannot be checked from these documents against a recast Q4 figure.

**Topics that disappeared or appeared**
- *Disappeared:* separate LinkedIn and Dynamics 365 growth rates (now folded into "Industry solutions cloud", +10%); the Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud and More Personal Computing segments.
- *Appeared:* the Agents and Infra and Devices and Consumer segments; Microsoft 365 commercial seats now include GitHub seats.

**Softer underlying trends** (some affected by the redefinitions)
- Microsoft 365 consumer cloud: +24% in Q4, +17% in Q1.
- Windows OEM and devices: −7% in Q4, −22% in Q1.
- Commercial RPO growth: 84% in Q4, 73% in Q1.

**Guidance mechanics are unchanged.** Both releases defer guidance to the call, and the forward-looking risk lists are effectively identical.

## 4. Analyst Q&A prep

1. **Capex doubled and FCF fell. When does capital intensity peak?**
   - *Draft answer:* "We will continue to invest in capital expenditures to support growth in our cloud offerings and our investments in AI training and other infrastructure."
   - Operating cash flow rose $12.9bn on higher customer receipts.
   - Existing resources are "sufficient to fund... material capital expenditures, for at least the next 12 months and thereafter."
   - *Source:* 10-Q MD&A, Liquidity.
2. **Commercial RPO was flat on the quarter and growth slowed from 84% to 73%. Why?**
   - *Draft answer:* RPO is $677bn, up 73%, with a weighted average duration of about 2.3 years and about 30% recognised in the next 12 months.
   - Q4 is seasonally stronger because of "a higher volume of multi-year contracts".
   - *Source:* 10-Q Note 11 and Seasonality; Q4 release.
3. **How dependent are you on OpenAI, and how much of RPO is OpenAI?**
   - *Draft answer:* OpenAI contributed $10.0bn of revenue in the quarter. Microsoft holds an approximately 24% equity-method stake, will "continue to receive revenue-sharing payments", and holds "rights to OpenAI's intellectual property". The offering is a "multi-model system".
   - The OpenAI share of RPO is not disclosed.
   - *Source:* 10-Q Note 1 and MD&A.
4. **Microsoft Cloud gross margin fell to 66%. Where is the floor?**
   - *Draft answer:* The decline was "driven by sales mix shift to Azure, offset in part by product-level margin improvements, primarily in Azure."
   - No floor is disclosed.
   - *Source:* 10-Q MD&A.
5. **Can a 49.8% operating margin last as depreciation rises from $7.1bn to $11.1bn?**
   - *Draft answer:* The margin was driven by "strong revenue growth, platform efficiency gains, and focused investments". Opex rose 6% and headcount fell 2%.
   - Management also says the investments "may decrease our operating margins."
   - *Source:* 10-Q MD&A.
6. **Is Azure's 48% growth comparable with last quarter's 43%, and are you capacity-constrained?**
   - *Draft answer:* The metric was updated "for the movement of certain components". Growth reflects "demand for services across the platform with continued growth across all workloads."
   - A like-for-like comparison is not given.
   - *Source:* 10-Q MD&A, Metrics.
7. **Why is the Copilot seat count no longer disclosed, and how is monetisation tracking?**
   - *Draft answer:* Microsoft 365 commercial cloud grew 17%, driven by "higher revenue per user... including Microsoft 365 Copilot, E5, and E7" and GitHub Copilot usage. Seats grew 5%.
   - The Q4 figure was "over 30 million paid seats".
   - *Source:* 10-Q MD&A; Q4 release.
8. **What are the $35.7bn of server-component receivables and the $12.6bn of supplier-restricted investments?**
   - *Draft answer:* They relate to "activities to facilitate the purchase of server components". Cash used in other investing rose $6.7bn "primarily to facilitate the purchase of components".
   - Counterparty terms are not disclosed.
   - *Source:* 10-Q Note 1 and MD&A.

## 5. Company brief

**What it does.** Microsoft sells cloud infrastructure, productivity software, AI services, advertising, gaming and devices.

**Segments** (from Q1 FY27):
- **Agents and Infra**, $76.8bn revenue and $40.6bn operating income this quarter. It covers:
  - Azure: compute, storage, GPU infrastructure and the Foundry AI platform.
  - Microsoft 365 cloud, including Copilot, GitHub and security products.
  - Server and Office licensing.
  - Industry solutions: Dynamics 365 and LinkedIn Talent and Sales Solutions.
  - Support services.
- **Devices and Consumer**, $15.5bn revenue and $5.4bn operating income. It covers search and advertising (Bing, Edge, LinkedIn Marketing), XBOX, and Windows OEM and devices.

**How it makes money.**
- Most revenue is recurring: subscriptions and Azure consumption.
- Microsoft Cloud is $63.9bn a quarter, and commercial backlog is $677bn.
- Revenue from OpenAI was $10.0bn in the quarter (10-Q).

**Strategy.**
- Build AI infrastructure at scale and add agents across its software.
- Offer a "multi-model system" while keeping OpenAI IP rights.
- Fund heavy capex ($39.4bn this quarter) from operating cash flow and leases.
- Grow the dividend: $0.98 a share, up from $0.91.

**Main risks** (10-Q Risk Factors):
- AI investment running ahead of revenue, with possible underutilisation and asset impairments.
- Shortages of power, land, permits and components.
- Concentration in partners who are also competitors, notably OpenAI.
- Margin dilution from the Azure mix.
- The IRS transfer-pricing claim of $28.9bn.
- AI regulation and competition-law scrutiny.
- Cybersecurity.
- Weak PC demand affecting Windows.

## 6. Questions to ask in an interview

1. Capex was $39.4bn against operating cash flow of $58.0bn, with $364.6bn of leases yet to commence. How do you think about returns on this capacity, and what would make you slow the build-out?
2. Commercial RPO was flat on the quarter at $677bn while growth slowed to 73%. Is that seasonal, or a sign that large AI contracts are lumpy? How should an outsider read backlog quality?
3. OpenAI was about 11% of revenue in the quarter, and the segment now describes a "multi-model system". How does the company balance that partnership against offering customers "choice"?
4. Microsoft Cloud gross margin fell to 66% on the Azure mix, yet operating margin reached 49.8%. Which efficiency levers are structural, and which are one-offs such as the Voluntary Retirement Program?
5. The $35.7bn of server-component receivables suggests Microsoft is effectively financing parts of its supply chain. How has supply-chain strategy changed with GPU scarcity, and what risks does it bring?
