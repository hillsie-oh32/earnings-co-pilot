# Apple Inc. (AAPL): Briefing Pack, FY2026 Q4 and Full Year

**Sources:** **Q4 PR** = earnings release filed 2 Nov 2026. **Q3 PR** = earnings release filed 30 Jul 2026. **10-K** = annual report for FY ended 26 Sep 2026. **Calc** = calculated numbers and red-flag scan. "My calc" means simple arithmetic on figures in these documents. Apple's fiscal year (FY) ends in late September, so Q4 is the July–September quarter.

## 1. Results summary

- **Growth slowed in Q4 but stayed double-digit.**
  - Q4 revenue was $114.5bn, up 11.7% (Calc; the Q4 PR rounds this to 12%). The prior three quarters grew 16–17%.
  - FY26 revenue was $478.8bn (+15.1%), and diluted EPS was $8.98 against $7.46 (+20%) (10-K).
  - Q4 growth by line (my calc, Q4 PR tables):

    | Grew | Shrank |
    |---|---|
    | iPhone +17.5% | iPad −10.4% |
    | Mac +23.7% | Wearables, Home and Accessories −7.9% |
    | Greater China +21% | Japan −1.4% |

  - Services grew only +9.8% in Q4, against +13% for the full year.
- **Margins rose, partly thanks to tariff refunds.**
  - FY gross margin (GM) was 48.8% against 46.9%. Products GM was 39.3% against 36.8%.
  - The 10-K attributes the products improvement "primarily [to] product mix and tariff refunds, partially offset by higher costs, including memory".
  - Q4 GM of 47.9% includes about 1 percentage point (pt) from refunds, and Q4 EPS of $2.10 includes $0.05 (Q4 PR).
  - Excluding refunds (my calc):
    - Q4 GM was about 46.9%, slightly *below* the 47.2% of a year earlier.
    - Q3 GM was about 48.1%, against 46.5% a year earlier.
    - This suggests memory costs are now outweighing mix gains.
- **AI spending is pushing up operating costs.**
  - R&D rose 32% to $45.8bn, about 10% of sales. The 10-K says this was driven by "higher infrastructure-related costs, including investments in artificial intelligence".
  - Q4 operating margin was 31.1% against 31.6% a year earlier, despite the refund benefit (Calc).
- **Cash flow was excellent for the year but softer in Q4.**
  - FY operating cash flow (OCF) was $145.1bn (+30.2%), 1.10x net income. Free cash flow (FCF) was about $136.1bn (sum of the Calc quarters), helped by capex falling to $9.0bn from $12.7bn.
  - Lower cash taxes also helped: $34.6bn against $43.4bn (10-K).
  - Q4 was weaker:
    - Q4 OCF was $28.1bn against $29.7bn a year earlier (my calc: 10-K full year minus Q3 PR nine months).
    - Q4 FCF was $25.9bn against $26.5bn (Calc).
- **Capital return was about $102bn.**
  - Buybacks were $86.4bn and dividends $15.7bn (10-K cash flow statement). The diluted share count fell 1.9%.
  - Apple also repaid $18.3bn of term debt and commercial paper, with no new issuance.
  - The dividend was held at $0.27 per quarter, and $114.0bn of buyback authorisation remains (10-K Item 5).
- **No guidance, and one unusual change.**
  - Neither release gives a numerical outlook. The 10-K warns that gross margins "will be subject to volatility and downward pressure" and that memory and storage cost trends are expected "to intensify".
  - **CEO change:** the Q3 PR quotes Tim Cook as CEO, while the Q4 PR quotes John Ternus. The 10-K lists Cook as Executive Chair. The documents give no date or rationale.

## 3. Red flags explained

### [RED] Inventory build-up: inventory days 9 → 22 (Calc)

**Verdict: largely explained and deliberate, but carries real risk. I would treat it as amber rather than red.**

**What happened:**
- Inventory rose from $5.7bn to $14.5bn.
- Almost all of the rise is **components**, which went from $2.1bn to $10.4bn. Finished goods rose only 14%, from $3.6bn to $4.1bn, which is slower than sales growth (10-K Note 7).
- The build was still under way in Q4: inventory was $11.1bn at June (Q3 PR).

**The innocent explanation in the documents (10-K):**
- Apple is "experiencing supply constraints and rising costs … for key components, including advanced semiconductors, memory and storage" and expects these "to intensify".
- A component build ahead of price rises is consistent with this.
- Q4 also saw major launches: iPhone Duo, iPhone 18 Pro, Mac mini, Mac Studio and Watch Series 12.
- Related movements point the same way:
  - Vendor non-trade receivables rose 20% to $39.9bn.
  - There was a $21.1bn cash outflow on other assets.
  - The 10-K refers to "prepayments associated with long-term supply agreements to secure supply".

**Bull reading:** Apple is using its balance sheet to lock in scarce memory supply. Finished goods remain lean.

**Bear reading:**
- The 10-K flags write-down and "purchase commitment cancellation risk".
- Manufacturing purchase obligations are $89.7bn, of which $82.7bn is due within 12 months.
- If memory prices fall or demand disappoints, the stockpile becomes a margin headwind.

### Calculated items worth clarifying

- **Beneish M-Score of −2.52** is *below* the −1.78 threshold, so it indicates **low** manipulation risk, despite the label wording in the scan.
- **Net debt of $23.4bn (Calc):**
  - This counts only cash and *current* marketable securities ($56.7bn) against $80.1bn of debt.
  - Including $80.2bn of non-current marketable securities, cash and securities total $136.9bn (10-K), giving **net cash of about $56.8bn** (my calc).
  - Either way, leverage is negligible.

## 4. Analyst Q&A prep

**1. "Excluding tariff refunds, Q4 gross margin was about 46.9%, below last year. How bad will memory costs get?"**
> "Products margin rose for the year on mix and tariff refunds, partially offset by higher costs, including memory. We are experiencing industry-wide supply constraints and expect these trends to intensify for memory and storage. We have taken, and may take, pricing actions, but in general margins will be subject to volatility and downward pressure." *(10-K MD&A and Gross Margin sections)*

**2. "How should we think about the December quarter given iPhone Duo and a tough comparison?"**
- Context: the prior-year December quarter was $143.8bn, up 15.7% (Calc).
> "We don't provide guidance in our release. We're entering the holiday season with our most ambitious lineup yet: iPhone Duo, iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, new home hardware and Touch on Mac, together with the all-new Siri AI. Our installed base reached an all-time high in every major category and region." *(Q4 PR)*
- Note: supply constraints on new products are also flagged in the 10-K.

**3. "How much more is there in tariff refunds, and what about the new Section 301 tariffs?"**
> "Refunds added about 1 point of gross margin and $0.05 of EPS in Q4, and about 2 points and $0.11 in Q3. We've applied for refunds through Customs processes and recognise them in products cost of sales as received. Further changes, including Section 232 semiconductor measures and Section 301 actions, remain possible, and we may not be able to collect all refunds." *(Q3 PR, Q4 PR, 10-K)*

**4. "R&D rose 32%, capex fell and licensed intangibles nearly doubled. What is the AI investment model?"**
> "R&D rose mainly due to infrastructure-related costs, including AI, and headcount. We use our own data centres and third-party cloud providers, and have made commitments to secure data centre capacity, components, equipment and energy. Intangible amortisation is expected to be $6.6bn in 2027." *(10-K MD&A, Risk Factors, Note 6)*
- Note: there is no disclosure on what was licensed or on Siri AI monetisation. It is bundled into device sales, with part of the revenue deferred (10-K Note 2).

**5. "Services slowed to about 10% growth in Q4. Is this regulation biting?"**
> "Services reached an all-time high, driven for the year by advertising and cloud services, with gross margin up to 76.2%." *(Q4 PR; 10-K)*
- Candidly, the 10-K flags the Epic injunction on link-out commissions (now at the Supreme Court), the Digital Markets Act and the Google search-remedy appeals as risks to Services revenue.

**6. "Why did inventory nearly triple, and what are the write-down risks?"**
> "Components drove the increase, ahead of our Q4 launches and amid tight memory and semiconductor supply. Finished goods rose only modestly. We review inventory and prepayments for recoverability and accrue cancellation reserves where needed." *(10-K Note 7; Risk Factors)*
- Manufacturing purchase obligations are $89.7bn (10-K).

**7. "Greater China grew 28% for the year. Is that sustainable?"**
> "Growth came primarily from iPhone and Mac, with a favourable renminbi. Segment operating income rose to $35.3bn from $26.9bn." *(10-K MD&A; Note 14)*
- No forward comment is available. The 10-K notes China data-localisation rules and trade risks.

**8. "With a new CEO, does capital allocation change?"**
> "We returned $28bn in Q4. We authorised a new $100bn buyback in April and raised the dividend to $0.27, and we intend to increase it annually. We believe $136.9bn of cash and securities, plus operating cash, covers our needs and capital return over the next 12 months and beyond." *(Q4 PR; 10-K Liquidity)*
- No statement on strategic change under the new CEO is available in these documents.

## 5. Company brief

**What it does.** Apple designs and sells consumer hardware, namely iPhone, Mac, iPad and Wearables/Home/Accessories, plus services sold to its installed base. Manufacturing is outsourced, mainly to China mainland, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam. About 60% of sales go through indirect channels (10-K).

**How it makes money.** FY26 revenue was $478.8bn:

| Line | Revenue | Share of sales |
|---|---|---|
| iPhone | $254.1bn | 53% |
| Services | $123.3bn | 26% |
| Mac | $37.9bn | — |
| Wearables, Home and Accessories | $35.6bn | — |
| iPad | $27.9bn | — |

- Services earn a 76.2% gross margin against 39.3% on products. That gives Services about 40% of gross profit despite only 26% of sales (my calc).
- Services include advertising (including search licensing with Google and others), AppleCare, cloud, the App Store and payments.
- Reported segments are geographic: Americas 41% of sales, Greater China 17%.

**Strategy.** An integrated hardware–software–services ecosystem. Regular launches (iPhone Duo, Siri AI) grow the installed base, which is then monetised through services. AI investment is heavy (R&D +32%), and nearly all FCF is returned through buybacks and a rising dividend.

**Main risks (10-K):**
- Memory and semiconductor cost inflation and supply constraints.
- Tariffs and a concentrated Asian supply chain.
- Regulatory pressure on App Store economics (Epic, Digital Markets Act, DOJ) and remedies affecting Google licensing.
- AI execution and access to compute.
- Dependence on iPhone.
- A CEO transition.

**Headcount.** About 167,000 full-time equivalents (10-K). The press-release boilerplate's "more than 150,000" is consistent but less precise.

## 6. Questions to ask in an interview

1. **Margins.** "Excluding tariff refunds, Q4 gross margin appears to have dipped below last year's, and the 10-K expects memory costs to intensify. How does Apple decide between absorbing component inflation and raising prices, given the warning that price rises 'may also reduce demand'?"
2. **AI model.** "Capex fell 29% while AI-driven R&D rose 32%, and licensed intangibles nearly doubled. How do you think about building versus renting or licensing AI capability, and how should investors assess returns on that spend?"
3. **Inventory as strategy.** "Component inventory rose roughly fivefold alongside $89.7bn of manufacturing purchase obligations. Is holding strategic component stock now a structural part of supply-chain management, and how is write-down risk governed?"
4. **Services durability.** "Services growth slowed to about 10% in Q4. With the Epic case at the Supreme Court and the Digital Markets Act investigations ongoing, how much can advertising and cloud, the cited growth drivers, offset pressure on App Store commissions?"
5. **Leadership.** "John Ternus's first results release leaned heavily on the hardware roadmap, while Tim Cook moved to Executive Chair. From the inside or as a close observer, what, if anything, has changed in priorities or decision-making since the transition?"
